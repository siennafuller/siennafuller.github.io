Undergraduate body of work
'The Rabbit Hole' (2021)

This body of work draws inspiration from a range of landscapes, focusing on colours, patterns, and textures that feel unnatural, heightened, or fantastical. During lockdown, with time expanding into stillness, the artist found refuge in her imagination, constructing fictional worlds as a form of escapism. These imagined realms became a way to reprocess the external environment, transforming ordinary scenery into something dreamlike and otherworldly.

Her practice navigates the space between reality and fantasy, intertwining both representational and abstract approaches to making. As the architect of these invented landscapes, she employs miniature porcelain, animalistic figures as symbolic instruments—devices that invite immersion, question perspective, and introduce intentional shifts in scale and juxtaposition. Through them, each scene becomes a site of both wonder and interrogation.

By reimagining natural environments through a surreal lens, the work reveals how deeply these landscapes have become warped and reshaped within her own internal world. What emerges is a series of idealised yet distorted impressions—worlds that exist somewhere between memory, imagination, and the desire to escape.

ONION GARDEN EXHIBITION
'Camp Fires' (2023)
Parian casting
6x6cm

This ceramic tea light holder presents a unique and playful concept that combines functionality with artistic expression, reminiscent of a cozy campfire setting. At its center is a small tea light candle, symbolizing the flickering warmth of a campfire, surrounded by miniature figurines in varying colors, each striking different poses.

The figurines evoke a sense of gathering around a campfire, suggesting themes of community, storytelling, and connection. Their varied postures—some standing, others seated—create an atmosphere of engagement, as if they are sharing stories or enjoying each other's company under the stars.

The use of different colors for the figures adds visual interest and could symbolize the diversity of voices and experiences that come together around a campfire. The overall design invites viewers to reflect on the warmth and camaraderie associated with campfire gatherings, transforming the simple tea light into a conversational piece that evokes feelings of nostalgia and togetherness.

This blend of functionality and creativity makes the tea light holder not just a decorative item, but a piece that encourages reflection on the nature of presence and the joy of communal experiences, much like those shared around a campfire

'Midnight Arbor' (2023)
'Spring Arbor' (2023)
'Amber Arbor' (2023)
Glass casting
29x25cm

Over the course of a year, Georgie's focus had been dedicated to exploring the possibilities of glass, specifically through casting and pâte de verre techniques.

These processes along with the innate qualities of the material had captured her interest, sparking an eagerness to delve deeper into self driven experimentation while discovering innovative ways to incorporate glass into their ceramic art.

Her exploration of glass has revolved around classical architecture and the influence of Escher's mind-bending graphics combined with the geometric style of Art Deco. This has informed her creative desire to approach the project as if it were a collage, something she enjoys in her spare time, using CAD to puzzle out shapes and assemble them in fun and unique ways. Her contemporary approach uses the translucent, ghostly qualities of glass to meditate on morphism and fluidity, creating a sense of dimension and transience.

These ambiguous forms allow viewers to glimpse nuances of the past or find echoes within memory.
